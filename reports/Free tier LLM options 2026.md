# Free tiers run one-call chess, not multi-agent

For a sample that makes one structured-JSON call per move, the only free option with a limit someone has seen directly is **Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite at 500 requests a day (user-supplied)**, which covers **about six single-call games a day**. The Gemini 3.6 and 3.8 Flash models, at **20 requests a day (user-supplied)**, cover one game every four days, which is not viable. Most provider pages could not be reached, so every other free figure is aggregator-only and none can be confirmed as a better baseline. For the 8-to-16-call multi-agent mode, **no free option reaches one full game a day on verified figures**. On aggregator numbers, Groq's general text models (**14,400 requests a day**) and Mistral's monthly token cap clear the bar, but Mistral's data terms exclude it, and paid tiers clear it as well. On data, **no free tier is suitable for player chat on current evidence**: Google's free tier may be used to improve products and read by human reviewers, Mistral's training terms are disputed, and OpenRouter's depend on each model's host. Anthropic is the only provider whose no-training clause was read on an official page, but its API is paid per token and its starter credit is small and unspecified. The sensible design keeps player chat off free keys, uses a free tier only for game-only move calls, and treats multi-agent mode as a paid or opt-in feature.

## One call per move fits Gemini; multi-agent fits no free tier

The arithmetic uses the notes' assumptions: 80 plies per game with one call per ply, and about 4,000 input plus 200 output tokens per call. If the model plays only one colour, the single-call figures double. A multi-agent game at 8 to 16 calls per ply is therefore 640 to 1,280 calls. The bar used here is one full multi-agent game a day, which is modest for a public demo. OpenRouter counts failed attempts toward its daily cap, so a retry loop spends the same budget as a fresh move. Every figure below is derived from the table above.

At one call per move, **Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite covers about six games a day**, and the cap is shared across everyone using the same Google Cloud project, per an aggregator ([ScriptByAI](https://www.scriptbyai.com/gemini-api-rate-limits/)). Its user-supplied **15 RPM** is tight for multi-agent play: a 16-call move needs about 64 seconds of pacing. The **5 RPM** on the 3.6 and 3.8 models stretches the same move to 3.2 minutes. Extra keys add no capacity, since the limits apply per project. Groq's general text models are the only free option whose reported figures clear the bar, at 11 to 22 multi-agent games a day. Those figures are aggregator-only, and one source says Groq moved to model-specific daily limits, so they need checking on the console. Groq's second model has a higher request cap, but its **200K tokens per day** binds first, at about 48 calls. OpenRouter's **50 requests a day** before a $10 purchase, or **1,000 after** (both aggregator-only), gives about 0.6 or 12 single-call games. Only the larger cap reaches a multi-agent game, and only at 8 calls per move, and its data policy rules it out anyway. Cerebras's trial **1M tokens a day** (aggregator-only) is about 240 calls, and its **5 RPM** puts a 16-call move at over three minutes. SambaNova's free tier has the same arithmetic as the 3.6 and 3.8 models. Cloudflare's **10,000 Neurons a day** cannot be converted to calls because the notes give no per-call Neuron cost.

Paid routes are what clear the multi-agent bar. SambaNova's Developer tier, at **20M tokens a day** (a search summary of official docs, not fetched), carries about 4,800 calls. For Anthropic, the lowest-priced current Claude model's official prices (**$0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output**, [pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) put each call near **$0.0005** under the notes' token assumption. That figure is an estimate from listed prices, not a measurement. A single-call game costs about $0.04 and a multi-agent game $0.32 to $0.64. The Start tier's **$500 monthly spend cap** ([rate limits](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)) covers roughly 26 to 52 multi-agent games a day. Once the cap is reached, requests fail until the next month, which is a real risk for a public game. New organizations may start in an Evaluation tier with lower, unpublished limits, so the Start-tier figures may not apply to a new account.

## Player chat has no free-tier home on current evidence

The move call is not automatically clean. A PGN carries name tags with player names, so they must be stripped before anything leaves the server. Player chat is personal data about third parties on any reading, and it must stay off free keys. Google's free tier is the clearest exclusion. Its terms preview says free-tier prompts and responses may be used to "provide, improve, and develop" products and that human reviewers may read them ([terms preview](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms_preview?hl=tr), snippet). Its billing page says paid-tier prompts are not used to improve Google products ([billing docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/billing?authuser=6), snippet), which implies the free tier is different. One blog says free prompts are not used for improvement ([Spur](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/is-gemini-api-free)), but it contradicts Google's terms and was set aside.

**Mistral's training premise is unresolved.** The brief's premise is that the free tier requires opting into training. One aggregator in the provider notes says the same ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/md/free-tier/mistral.md)). The data notes disagree. A help-article snippet and a secondary source describe default-on training with a console toggle to opt out ([help article](https://help.mistral.ai/en/articles/455207-can-i-opt-out-of-my-input-or-output-data-being-used-for-training); [Shieldra](https://www.shieldra.ai/ai-vendors/mistral-la-plateforme)). A secondary report also describes a March 12, 2026 change to the commercial terms for free subscribers ([Platform Governance Archive](https://www.platformgovernancearchive.org/2026/mistral-ai-broadens-scope-user-data-and-search-results-now-used-for-training/)). The help article itself was not reached. Both readings leave free-tier data open to training, and they differ only on whether the developer must opt in or opt out. Mistral stays excluded for player data until the primary help page and terms are read.

OpenRouter cannot be assessed as a whole, because each model's data policy follows its host and the free router changes models at random ([pricing snippet](https://openrouter.ai/pricing)). Cloudflare has the strongest free-tier stance in the notes, but it is seen only as a snippet of its official data-usage page ([data usage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/data-usage/)). A cross-tenant exposure on one model, reported by Knostic with no date, is a data-isolation concern to test ([Knostic](https://www.knostic.ai/blog/hallucination-or-cross-tenant-when-an-llm-answers-with-another-users-context)). Together's stance is strong on paper (training off by default, zero data retention at organization level), but its free tier is unclear. Groq's no-training line comes from an archived page, and its logs may persist for about 30 days ([Meetily](https://meetily.ai/llm-privacy/groq), secondary). DeepSeek's sources conflict on training and give no fixed retention period ([zdr.dev](https://zdr.dev/e/deepseek-api-firstparty)). Anthropic's Commercial Terms say it **may not train models on Customer Content** ([Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). Its API docs say prompts and outputs are **not retained by default**, with a 30-day retention rule for a separate set of top-tier models ([API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). Its Consumer Terms bar automated access except through an API key ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)), so a public app must call the API from its server with its own key.

## Keep the free tier for game data, and gate multi-agent

Split the sample's traffic in two. The move call should carry game data only: FEN, the legal-move list, and PGN with name tags removed. For that call, the Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite free tier is the sensible default, because its limits are the only ones seen directly. Sending game-only data to it is my judgement, not a finding in the notes, so the sample should say so. The six-games-a-day cap is the real constraint, and the sample should fall back gracefully when it is reached. Player chat should not touch any free key. The clean options are to disable chat in the free build, or to send chat to a paid key whose terms were read on official pages, which means Anthropic's API. Together with zero data retention enabled on a paid plan is the other candidate, pending confirmation. Cloudflare's no-training stance is the best free-tier fit for chat, but its daily pool cannot be sized until a test call measures Neurons per move.

Keep the streaming chat and the JSON move call as separate requests, because Groq's documents say streaming is not supported with Structured Outputs ([Groq structured outputs](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs.md), snippet). Use each provider's schema mode where it exists, but treat it as best-effort. Cloudflare's JSON mode can fail to meet the schema ([JSON mode](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/json-mode/), snippet), and Groq's strict mode is the only one described as constraining generation. Every provider still needs a code-side check that the move is legal, with at most one or two retries, because each retry spends daily quota. Mistral documents schema output ([custom structured outputs](https://docs.mistral.ai/capabilities/structured-output/custom_structured_output/), snippet), but its data terms exclude it for now.

For multi-agent mode, do not make a free tier the default. Gate it behind a user-supplied key or a paid tier with a stated monthly cap. If the sample must run it on a free tier, Groq's general text models are the only candidate that clears the bar, and only after their figures are confirmed on the Groq console. Before committing, run a local test on about 50 positions with three to five candidates, recording legal-move rate, schema validity and latency, and include the Gemini baseline, as the quality notes recommend.

## Quality cannot be ranked, and several caps remain unknown

No free model can be ranked on quality from this research, and this report makes no ranking claim. The one per-model index, Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite on Artificial Analysis, shows **12, 22 and 36** across three snippets, with no index version or date shown ([release page](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/releases/gemini-3-5-flash-lite); [model page](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-5-flash-lite); [Spanish page](https://artificialanalysis.ai/es/models/gemini-3-5-flash-lite)). The official Berkeley function-calling leaderboard snapshot is dated 2025-12-16, and no free-tier result was read from it ([leaderboard](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard)). A third-party LMArena table places one Gemini Flash Lite model near 1447 on WebDev, but it is unverified ([swfte](https://www.swfte.com/de/lmarena)). The chess research establishes a failure mode rather than a ranking. SPIN-Bench reports illegal moves becoming more common as games grow more complex ([arXiv 2503.12349](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12349), March 2025). ChessArena measures legal rates across more than a dozen models ([arXiv 2509.24239](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24239), September 2025), but no free-tier breakdown was read. No source tests whether general benchmarks predict chess legality, so legality checks and retries are required on every provider.

Several caps remain unknown. Cloudflare's per-call Neuron cost, Together's daily cap, Hugging Face's per-token prices and DeepSeek's limits were not found. Groq's sources conflict, and the per-minute and token caps for its smaller models were not found. SambaNova's free-tier RPM and RPD conflict across sources. The Anthropic starter credit amount is unpublished, and the Evaluation-tier limits are unpublished. Commercial-use and public-app terms were not found for any provider, and Anthropic's Usage Policy was not checked. Free access is also unstable. OpenRouter has deprecated free variants in batches, one dated July 2026 ([gist](https://gist.github.com/rlnorthcutt/e6f392cd1ffb1339cc42dfb024c3cf7f), secondary). Google has dated retirements for older Flash models ([deprecations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations.md)) and reportedly removed Pro models from its free tier in April 2026 ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/gemini-pricing/), secondary). Cerebras ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/free-tier/cerebras)), Together ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/free-tier/together)), SambaNova ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/llm-api-providers/sambanova-cloud/free-plan/)) and Hugging Face ([agentdeals](https://github.com/robhunter/agentdeals/issues/2732)) have each reportedly moved off standing free access.

## Conclusion

The useful shift is from asking which free provider has the best limits to asking which data path each message can take. On the figures reached, free tiers are a demo budget. One Gemini key covers about six single-call games a day, and no free route covers a full multi-agent game a day on verified numbers. Daily caps, not per-minute limits, decide the design, and retries draw on the same budget, which makes them a hidden cost. Multi-agent mode is therefore a paid feature in practice, and the sample should price it as one: an opt-in key with a stated monthly cap, not a default.

Two checks would settle the most. The Gemini daily cap is user-supplied, and it sets the default, so confirming it on the dashboard is a short step. The Groq daily figures are the only ones that would change the multi-agent answer, and they need the Groq console. The Mistral help article would settle the data question the notes leave open. None of these needs another aggregator search.
